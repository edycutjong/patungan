The kitty that pays the court, or pays everyone back.

Patungan: Indonesian for chipping in together. Picture Rina booking the Thursday 5-a-side football pitch and sharing one link; friends pay their seats in QUSDC or in QIE. The last seat pays the court owner in that same transaction. If the deadline passes first, everyone takes their QUSDC back.

Questions

What a judge would ask

Is it really on QIE mainnet?

Yes. The contract is 0x2f4646D52a5393E239f5b880411dC2CA2D945F31, source verified on Blockscout. Pool #1 was paid by its last seat and pool #2 was refunded after its deadline; every transaction is on mainnet.qie.digital, and /verify reads the contract live.

Who are the users?

None yet: 0 real users. Both mainnet pools ran from builder wallets, and the app labels them as builder wallets. Pool #1 paid the builder's own .qie name, not a seller. The next proof is a real group paying a real booking.

What if the group doesn't fill?

Then nobody is paid. After the deadline each member can take their QUSDC back with claimRefund, and anyone can call refundAll to return everyone's at once (in batches for large pools). The organizer can also cancel early, which opens the same refunds. A seat paid in QIE comes back as QUSDC.

Can the organizer run off with the money?

The organizer never holds it. QUSDC sits in the contract until the last seat, then goes to the payee fixed when the pool was created. Patungan has no admin key and no withdraw function. The one risk outside our code is QUSDC's issuer role, listed above.

Why swap QIE inside the join?

Members who hold QIE can pay without a separate swap. joinWithQIE buys exactly the seat price and returns unused QIE in the same transaction. It reverts if the swap would cost more than the Oracle's fair value plus a guard set at deploy (1,500 bps on the mainnet contract; the code caps it at 2,000).

What does the .qie name do?

The organizer types the payee as a name, such as pool #1's qkulk75527.qie. Patungan resolves it through Zone's domainOwnerByName at creation and stores the address. If the name points somewhere else by the time the transaction is mined, creation reverts with PayeeChanged.

Everyone chips in, or everyone gets their money back.
